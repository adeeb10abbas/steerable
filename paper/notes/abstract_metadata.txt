Directional failures in language-conditioned manipulation are often attributed to weak grounding. We test this explanation with matched LEFT/RIGHT trials. Across 324 pairs from eight completed checkpoint--arena rows, 253 endpoints (78%) shift in the requested direction, showing that language redirects control even when completion differs. Reflection reverses placement advantage in three checkpoints and flips the favored direction for pi0.5: LEFT/RIGHT completion changes from 4/27 and 25/27 to 25/27 and 9/27. A seven-position sweep changes the gap gradually, while a symmetric scene brings DreamZero's observed gap near zero. Canonical response does not guarantee transfer across language forms: expressing the same goals from the other object's frame collapses pi0.5 endpoint separation from 23.3 to 0.2 cm. Binary directional success is not a grounding test by itself: geometry can make responsive control look like failure, while familiar wording can make surface-form sensitivity look like semantic understanding.
