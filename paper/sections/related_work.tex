\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Generalist robot policies combine pretrained visual-language representations with expressive action decoders. Representative systems include $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$, which use flow-matching action models, and OpenVLA, an open autoregressive alternative~\cite{black2024pi0,black2025pi05,kim2024openvla}. Their scale has expanded the tasks addressable by one policy, but it also makes a failed episode difficult to attribute: language interpretation, object localization, grasping, transport, release, and task scoring all lie on the same path to success.

Counterfactual evaluations reduce this ambiguity by holding much of the visual input fixed while changing the instruction. LIBERO-CF and Counterfactual Action Guidance examine cases in which visual evidence dominates a conflicting instruction~\cite{fang2026cag}; CAST expands instruction diversity through counterfactual labels~\cite{glossop2025cast}; and Steerable Policies train controllers on task, motion, trace, and point commands~\cite{chen2026steerable}. These methods address genuine failures of instruction following. Our question is complementary: when the objects are already fixed and the requested relation changes, does a LEFT/RIGHT completion contrast uniquely diagnose language grounding? Target contact, for example, can verify object selection but cannot verify whether the target was placed on the requested side of the reference.

Manipulation benchmarks typically retain a frozen binary task predicate, as they should. DROID and RoboTwin illustrate the diverse data and simulation ecosystems used to train and test modern policies~\cite{khazatsky2024droid,mu2025robotwin}. A binary predicate answers whether the task was completed in the evaluated scene distribution. It can nevertheless saturate at ceiling, collapse at floor, or hide large continuous placement changes. We keep the original predicate as the final outcome, but pair it with signed endpoint and requested-depth measurements and with interventions that vary language form and scene geometry separately.
